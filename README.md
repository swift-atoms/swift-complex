# swift-complex

Use this package when you need complex numbers over a binary floating-point scalar with IEEE-careful arithmetic and principal-branch elementary functions. Start from `Complex.Number<Scalar>`, whose components are the distinct types `Complex.Real<Scalar>` and `Complex.Imaginary<Scalar>`. Arithmetic, square root, integer powers and the modulus need only `BinaryFloatingPoint` (they work for `Float16`); exponential, logarithmic, trigonometric, hyperbolic and complex-power functions also need the scalar to conform to `Trigonometry.Protocol` and `Exponential.Protocol`, and this package supplies that conformance for no scalar: the caller brings it.

## Laws

- Every multivalued function returns its principal value. On the negative real axis the sign of a zero imaginary part chooses the side of the branch cut: `Complex.Number.Math.sqrt()` of `(-4, +0)` is `2i` and of `(-4, -0)` is `-2i`; `Complex.Number.Math.Log` of `(-0, -0)` has imaginary part `-π`. `Complex.Number.Polar.phase` is `atan2(imaginary, real)` and keeps the same signed-zero distinction.
- The logarithm of zero is `-∞` in its real part, and `Complex.Number.Math.Log.One.plus()` of `-1` is likewise `-∞`; neither returns NaN.
- `Complex.Number.Magnitude` and `Complex.Number.Polar.length` rescale by the larger component's exponent, so they never overflow or underflow in an intermediate step, keep subnormal results, and agree with the platform `hypot` to within 2 ulp. An infinite component makes the result `+∞` even when the other component is NaN. `Complex.Number.Magnitude.squared`, `Complex.Number.Polar.squared` and `Complex.Number.reciprocal` do not rescale and can overflow or underflow; complex `/` rescales when the divisor's squared length is not normal, so `Complex.Number<Scalar>.one / z` is the safe reciprocal.
- Dividing by a zero `Complex.Number` returns `Complex.Number<Scalar>.infinity`, which is `(+∞, 0)`, whatever the numerator. Dividing by a `Complex.Real` or `Complex.Imaginary` divides each component by the scalar, so a zero divisor gives IEEE infinities and NaNs instead.
- `Complex.Number.isFinite` is false when either component is infinite or NaN, and `Complex.Number.description` writes every non-finite value, NaN included, as `inf`.
- `Complex.Number.Math.Exp`, `Complex.Number.Math.cosh()`, `Complex.Number.Math.sinh()` and `Complex.Number.Math.tanh()` evaluate large real parts through a split exponent, so a component that is finite in exact arithmetic stays finite when the other overflows, and the component signs follow the function's symmetry under negating the real part. `exp` of `(-∞, NaN)` is zero.
- `Complex.Number.Math.Exp.Minus.one()`, `Complex.Number.Math.Log.One.plus()` and `Complex.Number.Math.Cos.Minus.one()` stay accurate near zero, where `exp() - 1`, `log` of `1 + z` and `cos() - 1` lose their digits to cancellation.
- `Complex.Number.Math.Pow` with an `Int` exponent multiplies by repeated squaring, starting from `Complex.Number<Scalar>.one`, and takes a negative exponent as a power of `Complex.Number<Scalar>.one / z`. Any value to the power `0`, zero included, is `one`; zero to a negative power is `Complex.Number<Scalar>.infinity`.
- `Complex.Number.Math.Pow` with a complex exponent `w` is `exp(w · log z)`. For a zero base it is zero when the real part of `w` is positive and `Complex.Number<Scalar>.infinity` otherwise, so a zero exponent gives `infinity` here but `one` with an `Int` exponent.
- `Complex.Number.Math.root(_:)` of degree `0` is NaN in both components; degree `-1` is `one / z`, degree `2` is `sqrt()`, and other degrees are the principal root `exp(log z / n)`. Zero to a negative degree is `Complex.Number<Scalar>.infinity`.
- `Complex.Number.Equals.approximate` compares the Euclidean distance between the two values. Equal values (equal infinities included) match; otherwise a non-finite operand never matches. The values match when the distance is at most the absolute tolerance, or at most the absolute tolerance plus the relative tolerance times the larger of the two lengths, evaluated after rescaling so that overflow cannot produce a false match. A negative or NaN tolerance makes it return false rather than trap. `Complex.Number.Equals.Componentwise.approximate` instead checks each component separately against an absolute tolerance.
- `Complex.Number.normalized` is nil for zero and for non-finite values; a subnormal value is rescaled first, so it still normalizes.
- `Complex.Number` encodes as an unkeyed container of two scalars, real then imaginary, and the Codable conformances are absent under Embedded Swift.
